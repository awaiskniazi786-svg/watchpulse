10 Best Korean Crime & Thriller Series on Netflix & Streaming (Beyond Squid Game, 2026)

By Alex Mercer • Editorial Guide • Updated November 26, 2026

Quick Answer: Why Korean Crime Series Outperform Endless Western Police Shows

"Tired of 7-season American network crime shows that stall out after Season 2? Korean thriller series design the entire 8-to-16 episode story before Episode 1 even airs—every clue in the premiere matters in the finale."

Many global viewers assume K-dramas only mean pastel romantic comedies—until they watch Squid Game or Parasite and realize South Korean television produces some of the darkest, sharpest detective mysteries and crime thrillers on streaming. Below are ten elite Korean thriller series split between 8-Episode Weekend Action & Revenge Sprints and 12-to-16 Episode Procedural Masterpieces, complete with episode commitments and tonal notes.

Part 1: Compact 7-to-8 Episode Netflix Thrillers (Finish in One Weekend)

1. My Name (2021) — 8 Episodes

Key Details: 8 Episodes (~45–55 min) • Platform: Netflix • Starring: Han So-hee, Park Hee-soon • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Witnessing her father gunned down outside her apartment door, Yoon Ji-woo rejects police delays and joins Seoul's largest drug syndicate to learn to fight—only to be ordered years later by her cartel boss to infiltrate the narcotics division as an undercover rookie cop. Think The Departed meets John Wick in crisp, rain-slicked neon corridors, anchored by a ferocious physical turn from Han So-hee.

Pacing Verdict: Fast, brutal, and completely self-contained in under seven hours. Expect intense hand-to-hand knife choreography and genuine plot betrayal rather than slow-burn dialogue.

2. Bloodhounds (2023–2026) — 8-Episode Season 1

Key Details: 8 Episodes (~50–60 min) • Platform: Netflix • Starring: Woo Do-hwan, Lee Sang-yi • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Two principled young amateur boxers team up with an aging ex-moneylender to take down a ruthless loan-shark empire preying on small café owners buried in pandemic debt. What elevates Bloodhounds above standard revenge stories is its heartwarming bromance—you genuinely root for the two leads even when they're battered inside jaw-dropping corridor brawl choreography.

3. Mask Girl (2023) — 7 Episodes

Key Details: 7 Episodes (~50–63 min) • Platform: Netflix • Director: Kim Yong-hoon • Starring: Go Hyun-jung, Ahn Jae-hong, Nana • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Structured like an anthology of shifting perspectives—where three different actresses play the same hunted woman across different chapters of her life—Mask Girl follows an insecure office clerk who becomes a viral masked livestreamer by night before an obsessive coworker drags her into an irreversible crime. Razor-sharp dark satire on internet parasocial culture.

4. Kingdom (2019–2020) — 12 Episodes Across 2 Crisp Seasons

Key Details: 12 Episodes (6 per Season) • Platform: Netflix • Writer: Kim Eun-hee • Starring: Ju Ji-hoon, Bae Doona • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Forget every tired zombie show you've quit. Set during Korea's Joseon dynasty, Crown Prince Lee Chang investigates a mysterious plague turning the dead king—and starving villagers—into fast-moving horrors while corrupt royal clans plot a palace coup. Written by Signal creator Kim Eun-hee, the political scheming is every bit as gripping as the sword-and-arrow survival sequences.

Part 2: Deep 12-to-16 Episode Detective Mysteries & Psychological Thrillers

5. Stranger / Secret Forest (2017–2020) — 16 Episodes per Season

Key Details: Season 1 Complete 16-Ep Arc • Platform: Netflix • Starring: Cho Seung-woo, Bae Doona • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Widely ranked among the smartest legal-crime mysteries ever written for television. After childhood brain surgery strips away his capacity for normal emotional warmth, brilliant prosecutor Hwang Si-mok relies strictly on cold logic—making him immune to political bribes. Paired with warm, observant detective Han Yeo-jin, he traces a routine murder back to systemic corruption at the highest prosecutor's office.

Why It Stands Apart: Zero romance subplot, zero soap-opera tropes, and Baeksang Grand Prize writing where almost every character has plausible, multi-layered motives.

6. Signal (2016) — 16 Episodes

Key Details: 16 Episodes • Genre: Time-Slip Cold Case Crime • Creator: Kim Eun-hee • Starring: Lee Je-hoon, Kim Hye-soo, Cho Jin-woong • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: If you loved the tense frequency-puzzle vibe of Frequency or Dark, stop everything and queue Signal. A disillusioned modern criminal profiler discovers an old battery-less police walkie-talkie that transmits at 11:23 PM—connecting him directly to a stubborn, big-hearted detective working real historical cold cases in 1989. Inspired by famous real-life Korean unsolved crimes (including the Hwaseong case featured in Memories of Murder), every changed detail in the past dangerously rewrites 2015.

7. Through the Darkness (2022) — 12 Episodes

Key Details: 12 Episodes • Genre: True-Crime Profiling Drama • Starring: Kim Nam-gil • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: South Korea's direct answer to Netflix's Mindhunter. Based on the non-fiction memoir by Korea's very first criminal profiler Kwon Il-yong, Song Ha-young fights dismissive late-1990s Seoul police brass who insist serial murder "doesn't happen in Korea"—forcing himself to sit across from locked-up killers to map their psychology before the next victim vanishes. Sober, understated, and deeply respectful toward real victims.

8. Beyond Evil (2021) — 16 Episodes

Key Details: 16 Episodes • Baeksang Best Drama Winner • Platform: Netflix • Starring: Shin Ha-kyun, Yeo Jin-goo • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: Set in the tight-lipped provincial town of Manyang, an eccentric local sergeant suspected twenty years earlier of murdering his own twin sister is forced to partner with an arrogant Seoul rookie investigator when identical serial killings resume. Everyone in the small town—including both detectives—hides secrets behind warm smiles, turning Episode 1 through 7 into one of television's finest paranoid chess matches.

9. The Glory (2022–2023) — 16 Episodes

Key Details: 16 Episodes (Parts 1 & 2 Complete) • Platform: Netflix • Writer: Kim Eun-sook • Starring: Song Hye-kyo, Lim Ji-yeon • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: A relentlessly methodical psychological revenge saga rather than a shoot-'em-up. Eighteen years after surviving horrific high-school bullying while teachers and wealthy parents looked away, Moon Dong-eun spends over a decade patiently turning herself into an elementary-school teacher assigned to her primary tormenter's young daughter—dismantling every accomplice's marriage, career, and cover story piece by piece.

Content Note: Episode 1 contains harsh scenes of school violence that establish the stakes; from Episode 2 onward it becomes icy, razor-sharp cat-and-mouse plotting.

10. Flower of Evil (2020) — 16 Episodes

Key Details: 16 Episodes • Genre: Domestic Hitchcockian Suspense • Starring: Lee Joon-gi, Moon Chae-won • Open Series Details →

Editorial Analysis: For fourteen years, metalworker Baek Hee-sung has played the ideal quiet husband and doting father—while his homicide detective wife unknowingly begins investigating a brutal copycat serial case tied directly to his erased teenage identity. Combining Hitchcockian domestic dread with emotional heartbreak, Lee Joon-gi delivers an astonishing tightrope performance as a man genuinely trying to love despite a terrifying past.

Frequently Asked Questions About Korean Thriller Series

What are the best short Korean thriller series on Netflix without slow romance filler?

Start with My Name (8 episodes), Bloodhounds (8 episodes), Mask Girl (7 episodes), or Kingdom (12 episodes across two fast seasons). All four skip cheesy filler entirely and maintain movie-grade pacing from Episode 1.

Which Korean crime show is closest to Mindhunter or True Detective?

Through the Darkness mirrors Mindhunter almost beat-for-beat inside late-1990s South Korea, while Beyond Evil and Stranger match the bleak atmosphere and razor-sharp writing of early True Detective.

Should I watch Korean series with subtitles or English dubbing?

Original Korean audio with English subtitles delivers far sharper emotional nuance—especially in interrogation rooms like Stranger and Beyond Evil—while Netflix's dubbed tracks for action series like My Name work smoothly if you watch late at night.
