Beyond Parasite: 7 Essential Korean & International Thrillers With Unforgettable Twists

By Alex Mercer • Editorial Guide • Updated November 21, 2026

Why International Thrillers Hit Harder Than Standard Studio Formulas

"I loved Parasite and Squid Game, and I'm tired of predictable Hollywood plot armor where you know the detective will win nicely in Act 3. What international thrillers should I watch next?"

Hollywood studio thrillers are often focus-grouped to protect likable stars from irreversible mistakes. By contrast, the best South Korean, Spanish, and Indian mysteries allow flawed protagonists to dig themselves into genuinely shocking moral corners—blending dark humor, social satire, and structural twists that stay with you for weeks.

1. Memories of Murder (2003) — South Korea (131 Minutes)

Key Details: 131 min • Language: Korean (ko) • Director: Bong Joon-ho • Starring: Song Kang-ho • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Sixteen years before Parasite, Bong Joon-ho directed this procedural masterpiece based on South Korea's first confirmed serial murders in 1986 Hwaseong. Two rural detectives with clumsy methods clash with a forensics-minded detective from Seoul as rain, bureaucracy, and technological limits sabotage every lead.

Why It Stands Out: If you love David Fincher's Zodiac (2007), Memories of Murder is its direct tonal sibling—and its final freeze-frame look into the camera is one of the haunting endings in cinema history.

2. The Invisible Guest / Contratiempo (2016) — Spain (106 Minutes)

Key Details: 106 min • Language: Spanish (es) • Director: Oriol Paulo • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: A wealthy Barcelona tech entrepreneur wakes up in a hotel room locked from the inside next to his lover's body. He has exactly three hours with a veteran defense strategist to rehearse his alibi before a key witness testifies—forcing them to replay the mountain-road cover-up from four conflicting angles.

Verdict: A razor-sharp locked-room puzzle that became so beloved globally it inspired multiple international remakes (including the hit Hindi thriller Badla).

3. The Call (2020) — South Korea (112 Minutes)

Key Details: 112 min • Language: Korean (ko) • Director: Lee Chung-hyun • Netflix Original • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Two women living in the exact same country house—one in 2019, the other in 1999—are connected through an old cordless landline phone. What starts as a heartwarming time-travel friendship turns into a terrifying cat-and-mouse war when the woman in 1999 realizes she can rewrite the 2019 caller's entire life and family history at will.

Tonal Note: Intense psychological thriller with dark violence; Jeon Jong-seo delivers an unforgettable villain performance.

4. Andhadhun (2018) — India (139 Minutes)

Key Details: 139 min • Language: Hindi (hi) • Director: Sriram Raghavan • Starring: Ayushmann Khurrana, Tabu • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: A piano player in Pune who pretends to be blind to sharpen his musical focus is hired to play a private concert at a retired actor's apartment—and accidentally "witnesses" the disposal of a murder victim while forced to keep playing the piano as if he sees nothing.

Verdict: Wickedly funny, unpredictable black-comedy crime thriller that twists five times after you think the story is over.

5. Burning (2018) — South Korea (148 Minutes)

Key Details: 148 min • Language: Korean (ko) • Director: Lee Chang-dong • Starring: Yoo Ah-in, Steven Yeun • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Adapted from a Haruki Murakami short story, an aspiring writer reconnects with a childhood neighbor who returns from Africa with a wealthy, enigmatic stranger (Steven Yeun in a chilling, charismatic performance). When the stranger casually confesses a bizarre hobby during sunset, a slow-burn obsession begins.

Audience Fit: A deliberate, atmospheric slow burn (148 min)—save this for a night when you want literary tension rather than fast chases.

6. Forgotten (2017) — South Korea (109 Minutes)

Key Details: 109 min • Language: Korean (ko) • Director: Jang Hang-jun • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: After a young man's idolized older brother is abducted before his eyes and returns 19 days later with no memory of where he went, small inconsistencies in their new house suggest the man who came back—and the family around him—are hiding something enormous.

Verdict: Do not read any plot summaries before pressing play; the midpoint reveal completely changes the genre of the film.

7. Tell No One / Ne le dis à personne (2006) — France (131 Minutes)

Key Details: 131 min • Language: French (fr) • Director: Guillaume Canet • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Eight years after a pediatrician's wife was believed murdered by a serial killer, he receives an encrypted email with a live webcam feed showing her looking back at him in a crowd—just as police dig up new evidence implicating him. Features one of the best foot-chase sequences across Paris highway traffic ever filmed.
