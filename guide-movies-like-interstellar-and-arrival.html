What to Watch If You Loved Interstellar & Arrival: 8 Smart Sci-Fi Films Compared

By Awais K • Availability Last Checked: November 19, 2026

Why Generic "Sci-Fi Recommendations" Usually Disappoint

Viewer Question Answered: "I want a sci-fi movie with the emotional gravity of Interstellar (2014) or the quiet intellectual wonder of Arrival (2016)—not another loud laser-battle blockbuster. Which film should I actually pick?"

Automated recommendation engines often lump Interstellar and Arrival together with superhero space opera simply because they share a "Sci-Fi" genre tag. What truly connects Christopher Nolan's wormhole odyssey and Denis Villeneuve's linguistic first-contact drama is parent-child sacrifice across time paired with awe-inspiring scientific mystery.

Below, we divide eight essential follow-ups into two clear lanes so you can pick the exact tone you want tonight.

Lane A: If You Loved the First-Contact Wonder & Ideas of Arrival

1. Contact (1997) — 150 Minutes

Key Details: 150 min • Rated PG • Director: Robert Zemeckis • Based on Carl Sagan's novel • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: Before Arrival asked how humanity would translate an alien syntax, Contact asked what happens when a SETI radio astronomer (Jodie Foster) actually detects a prime-number pulse from Vega—and how politics, religion, and science collide before a single human leaves Earth.

Editorial Verdict: Slightly dated 1990s broadcast-news cameos, but its emotional core and dedication to scientific curiosity make it Arrival's closest spiritual predecessor.

2. Annihilation (2018) — 115 Minutes

Key Details: 115 min • Rated R • Director: Alex Garland • Starring: Natalie Portman • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: A cellular biologist enters "The Shimmer," a quarantined coastal anomaly where DNA refracts like light through a prism. Like Arrival, it treats alien presence as something genuinely incomprehensible rather than an army waiting to be shot.

Limitations: Features two intense body-horror sequences (including a notorious mutated bear scene), so it is far darker and more unsettling than Arrival.

3. Ex Machina (2014) — 108 Minutes

Key Details: 108 min • Rated R • Director: Alex Garland • Starring: Alicia Vikander, Oscar Isaac, Domhnall Gleeson • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: Replaces extraterrestrials with artificial consciousness. A young coder is flown to a secluded Norwegian estate to administer a face-to-face Turing test to an AI named Ava. Just as Arrival revolves around glass-walled conversation sessions, Ex Machina builds unbearable suspense out of seven quiet interviews.

4. Nope (2022) — 130 Minutes

Key Details: 130 min • Rated R • Director: Jordan Peele • Starring: Daniel Kaluuya, Keke Palmer • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: Combines mystery first-contact spectacle with IMAX cinematography by Hoyte van Hoytema (who also shot Interstellar). Approaches an unidentified aerial phenomenon through the lens of animal behavior and spectacle.

Lane B: If You Loved the Deep-Space Scale & Isolation of Interstellar

5. The Martian (2015) — 144 Minutes

Key Details: 144 min • Rated PG-13 • Director: Ridley Scott • Starring: Matt Damon • Open Title Page & Providers →

Why It Connects: Funnily enough, Matt Damon goes from playing a marooned astronaut in Interstellar to the ultimate optimistic marooned astronaut on Mars a year later. Where Interstellar leans into metaphysical grief and relativity, The Martian celebrates practical engineering, botany, and global cooperation.

Editorial Verdict: The most crowd-pleasing, re-watchable hard sci-fi film of the last 15 years.

6. Ad Astra (2019) — 123 Minutes

Key Details: 123 min • Rated PG-13 • Director: James Gray • Starring: Brad Pitt • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: Shot by Interstellar cinematographer Hoyte van Hoytema, Ad Astra sends an astronaut from the Moon to Mars and finally to Neptune in search of his missing father. It mirrors Interstellar's parent-child ache, though in a much slower, meditative, internal tone.

7. Sunshine (2007) — 107 Minutes

Key Details: 107 min • Rated R • Director: Danny Boyle • Written by Alex Garland • Starring: Cillian Murphy • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: In 2057, a crew aboard the spacecraft Icarus II carries a stellar payload to reignite a dying Sun. Its first two acts of orbital physics, blinding solar shields, and crew sacrifice clearly influenced modern prestige space cinema, paired with one of John Murphy's greatest film scores.

8. Moon (2009) — 97 Minutes

Key Details: 97 min • Rated R • Director: Duncan Jones • Starring: Sam Rockwell • Check Live Regional Streaming →

Why It Connects: Sam Rockwell plays a solitary lunar miner nearing the end of a three-year contract harvesting helium-3 on the far side of the Moon when an accident exposes the truth about his mission. Proves that profound sci-fi doesn't need a $200M budget.
