8 Practical Action Movies Like John Wick (Real Stunts, Clean Editing & Zero Shaky Cam)

By Awais K • Editorial Guide • Updated November 21, 2026

Why John Wick Changed Modern Action Cinema

"I loved the fluid combat, clear wide shots, and stunt precision of the John Wick franchise—which other action movies actually let you see the choreography instead of cutting every half-second?"

For over a decade, Hollywood disguised untrained actors with zoomed-in shaky cameras and twenty quick cuts to hide a single punch. Directed by former stunt coordinators Chad Stahelski and David Leitch, John Wick revived Hong Kong action discipline: wide framing, longer takes, physical exhaustion, and clear spatial geography so you always understand where every fighter, doorway, and weapon sits. Every title below shares that genuine stunt-first DNA.

1. The Raid: Redemption (2011) — The Modern Gold Standard of Martial Arts Action

Key Details: 101 min • Director: Gareth Evans • Language: Indonesian (id) • Starring: Iko Uwais, Yayan Ruhian • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: An elite Jakarta SWAT team storms a dilapidated 30-story apartment tower run by a crime lord—and gets trapped inside once the exits lock shut. Gareth Evans and fight stars Iko Uwais and Yayan Ruhian execute jaw-dropping Indonesian Pencak Silat sequences at blinding speed while keeping the camera wide and legible. Virtually every major Western action director of the last fifteen years cites The Raid as an influence.

2. Nobody (2021) — John Wick's Scrappier, Funnier Cousin

Key Details: 92 min • Director: Ilya Naishuller • Written by Derek Kolstad (John Wick creator) • Starring: Bob Odenkirk • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Written by the screenwriter of John Wick and produced by David Leitch, Nobody casts Bob Odenkirk as a seemingly dull suburban dad whose quiet routine snaps after a home burglary. What separates Hutch Mansell from John Wick is rustiness: in the film's signature city-bus brawl, he takes brutal hits, gets winded, and improvises clumsily before muscle memory kicks back in. Lean, funny, and ruthlessly paced at 92 minutes.

3. Atomic Blonde (2017) — Exhausting, Realistic Close-Quarters Combat

Key Details: 115 min • Director: David Leitch • Starring: Charlize Theron, James McAvoy • Setting: 1989 Berlin • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Set days before the fall of the Berlin Wall, MI6 agent Lorraine Broughton hunts a stolen list of double agents across neon-drenched Cold War alleys. While the espionage plot gets tangled, the action is extraordinary—highlighted by an extended apartment-and-stairwell brawl where Charlize Theron and stunt fighters grow visibly exhausted, leaning on walls to catch their breath between desperate blows.

4. Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) — Visual Storytelling at 70 Miles Per Hour

Key Details: 120 min • Director: George Miller • 6 Academy Awards • Starring: Tom Hardy, Charlize Theron • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: George Miller famously center-framed every crucial action beat across more than 2,700 cuts so your eyes never lose track of a single leap, harpoon, or rolling tanker rig. Built around real custom vehicles roaring across the Namibian desert with stunt performers swinging on towering flexible poles, Fury Road proves practical physics still obliterate weightless green-screen CGI.

5. Mission: Impossible – Fallout (2018) — Escalating Practical Set Pieces

Key Details: 147 min • Director: Christopher McQuarrie • Starring: Tom Cruise, Henry Cavill, Rebecca Ferguson • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Few modern franchises match Fallout's insistence on doing stunts for real: a genuine HALO skydive at sunset, Tom Cruise piloting an actual helicopter through New Zealand canyons, and a bone-rattling Paris bathroom fight against Liang Yang where Henry Cavill memorably "reloads" his fists. Every sequence escalates cleanly with crystal-clear spatial stakes.

6. Extraction (2020) — Stunt-Coordinator Camera Mastery

Key Details: 116 min • Director: Sam Hargrave • Starring: Chris Hemsworth, Rudhraksh Jaiswal, Randeep Hooda • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Directed by veteran Marvel stunt coordinator Sam Hargrave—who strapped himself to the hood of moving cars to operate the camera personally—Extraction follows a mercenary hired to rescue a kidnapped teenager across Dhaka. Its centerpiece 12-minute seamless-cut car chase and apartment brawl delivers ferocious close-quarters choreography that rivals theatrical blockbusters.

7. Monkey Man (2024) — Raw Revenge Energy With Cultural Mythos

Key Details: 121 min • Director & Star: Dev Patel • Action & Thriller • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Dev Patel wears director, co-writer, and star hats in this gritty Mumbai-set revenge thriller about an underground bare-knuckle fighter infiltrating a corrupt political elite. While rougher around the edges than John Wick's glossy minimalism, Monkey Man compensates with fierce emotional conviction, inventive close-quarters brawls, and electrifying tabla-driven training sequences.

8. Bullet Train (2022) — Stylishly Chaotic Assassin Banter

Key Details: 126 min • Director: David Leitch • Starring: Brad Pitt, Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Brian Tyree Henry, Hiroyuki Sanada • Open Title Details & Trailer →

Editorial Analysis: Five quirky assassins discover their assignments overlap aboard a speeding Tokyo-to-Kyoto Shinkansen. If your favorite aspect of the John Wick sequels is the eccentric assassin underworld and creative prop fights inside tight spaces, Bullet Train delivers colorful neo-noir wit and nimble physical comedy—including Brad Pitt fighting over a briefcase while trying not to wake passengers in the Quiet Car.

One Quick TV Setting Fix Before You Press Play

Before streaming high-speed practical action on a modern smart TV, open your TV's Picture Settings and turn OFF "Motion Smoothing" (often labeled TruMotion, Auto Motion Plus, or Motionflow). Artificial frame interpolation destroys 24fps cinematic motion blur, making crisp martial arts stunts look strangely sped-up like daytime soap operas.
